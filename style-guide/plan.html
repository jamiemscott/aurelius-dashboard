Style Guide

Plan My Future

The scripted planning chat at /chat/plan-my-future. The chat reuses adv-* from modules/adviser.css; the plan panel lives in modules/plan.css and its Highcharts 13 styling in modules/plan-chart.css. All new classes are prefixed plan-.

Layout & drawer

.plan-layout is a centred two-column grid, at most 1560px wide: the chat (.chat-wrap.plan-chat) takes a third and the plan (aside.plan-panel[popover]) two thirds. The chat is sticky, and its message log is the only inner scroll area. The plan never scrolls on its own: it grows with its content and the page scrolls, which avoids nested scrollbars. Once the panel is 720px or wider, .plan-panel-body uses a container query for a 2fr / 1fr grid: the period pills and KPIs run across the top, the chart/table flip card takes two thirds with the allocation doughnut in the last third, and holdings, changes and assumptions run full width below. Holdings rows flow into as many 320px columns as fit. Above 1080px, author CSS overrides the browser’s [popover] hiding so the panel sits inline. Below 1080px it is hidden until the View plan button (popovertarget) opens it as a right-hand drawer. Focus handling, Escape to close and the backdrop come from the browser, with no script.

Period pills

These look like .range-pill but are real radio buttons inside a fieldset, so arrow keys and screen-reader grouping work with no script. The periods extend the site’s 1M · 3M · 6M · 1Y forward to 3Y, 5Y and All (25 years, the plan left to grow). Targets are at least 24px tall.

Chart ⇄ table flip card

The projection and the allocation each show as a chart or a table on the two faces of one card. A Chart/Table radio pair (styled like the period pills) inside a .plan-flippable section turns its .plan-flip-inner over with :has() and a 3D rotate, with no script. Both faces share one grid cell, so the card keeps the height of the taller face and nothing below it jumps. Each face’s visibility swaps at the half-turn, so the hidden face can’t take focus or be read. Reduced motion swaps the faces instantly; forced-colours mode gives each face a system border.

Holdings rows & badges

.plan-line rows show each holding’s status with data-status (kept, added or removed). Each row has a unique view-transition-name, so adding or removing a holding animates through the View Transitions API (turned off with reduced motion). The remove and undo buttons work without the chat. Their accessible names include the holding’s name.

Add more. Every holding except cash has a + button (“Add more to …”). It answers in the chat with up to three option cards: a one-off amount of new money, a bigger monthly amount, or some of your cash. In an ISA the one-off amount is capped at the allowance left, and the cash option only appears if at least £10,000 would stay in cash. The buttons sit together in .plan-line-actions. After placing orders, a top-up shows as one new Buy order.

Option cards

Choices in the chat always come as three .plan-option cards, each with plus and minus points, so the trade-off is visible before anything is picked. They're used for the approach (more cautious, balanced, more adventurous) and then for three funds that suit it. Fund cards add the key figures. The signs are CSS decoration with empty alt text (content: "+" / ""); screen readers hear “Plus:” and “Minus:” from hidden text instead. Each button's name includes the option, e.g. “Add to plan Vanguard S&P 500”. Once one is picked it gets a gold outline and a badge, and the other buttons are removed. The other cards keep full contrast rather than fading. The copy explains and never ranks: no option is labelled “best”.

Weighing it up

Four patterns help the user test a plan before acting on it.

Preview. A fund option that isn't in the plan yet says what it would add: .plan-option-preview, worked out with previewAdd() on a copy of the plan. Hovering or focusing the card draws it on the projection chart as a dashed “With …” line, and leaving the card clears it. The dashed line is extra: the sentence gives the same figure to everyone, and the hover hint is hidden on touch screens.

Stress test. “What if markets fall 25%?” applies the fall to each holding by its sensitivity (gold, gilts and cash fall less), then works out how long a recovery would take at the assumed growth, with monthly amounts still going in. The bars are decoration (aria-hidden); the figures sit in a <dl>.

Quick check. The first review asks three yes/no questions in .plan-check, one <fieldset> each. A “No” never blocks: it explains the concern, and the review then carries a .plan-review-concern note pointing to James. Continuing with a question unanswered moves focus to it, with a polite hint.

Ask about. A <details> above the message box lists common questions (costs, market falls, ISAs, pensions, cash, property, shares, crypto, timing) as chips. They can also be typed. Answers explain and never recommend, and crypto carries the FCA's warning.

Review and invest

.plan-review is how the chat turns a plan into a decision. It lists every order (sells first, then buys, then monthly amounts), each tagged with its account. Below that come the first-year costs in pounds, the allowance use, and links to the Key Information Documents.

Placing orders needs two ticks. Until both are ticked, the button stays focusable with aria-disabled="true" rather than disabled, so keyboard and screen-reader users can still reach it and learn why it isn't ready. Pressing it moves focus to the first unticked box, and a polite live hint explains what's missing. New money with no account chosen also blocks it.

Make this investment. Once a fund is chosen, a .plan-invest-bar between the conversation and the message box jumps straight to the review from any point. In the narrow drawer the same button sits in the plan's header and closes the drawer first. If the new money has no account yet, it asks for one before the quick check. It steps aside while the latest reply shows the check or review, hides once the orders are placed, and comes back when the plan changes. Typing “make this investment” or “place my orders” does the same.

2 orders ready

After the decision, the card closes with a badge: “Orders placed”, “Sent to James” or “Saved for later”. If the plan changes instead, the card says it's out of date. The note makes the execution-only position plain. A reply taller than the log scrolls to its start, and the review takes focus as a whole.

Allowances

The plan panel shows this tax year's ISA and pension allowances as native <meter> elements, styled with the WebKit and Firefox pseudo-elements. Each meter is labelled by its title and described by the figures underneath, so screen readers hear the numbers and not just a bar. Near the limit the bar uses the browser's sub-optimum state, and past the limit it turns red and says how much is over. Holdings show their account, and new money still waiting for one reads “Account to choose”. Demo accounts match the other pages: an Advised SIPP, an ISA with £8,240 left, and Veritas and cash in the general account.

Highcharts token bridge

Charts use Highcharts 13.0.0, the version OutSystems Charts (ODC) 3.12 ships, in styledMode. Highcharts outputs only class names. .plan-chart maps our tokens onto its --highcharts-color-N and --highcharts-neutral-color-* variables. It also resets Highcharts’ own color-scheme: light dark to inherit, so charts follow the site toggle rather than the OS. Each series colour reaches at least 3:1 against the card surface. Forced-colours mode switches to system colours.

Accessibility

How the page handles screen readers, keyboards and display settings.

  • The conversation is a role="log". Only finished replies are added to it, and the typing indicator is aria-hidden.
  • Each change to the plan is announced once in a separate polite status region, outside the drawer so it’s heard even when the drawer is closed.
  • The Highcharts accessibility module gives the charts keyboard navigation and screen-reader descriptions. Both the projection and allocation cards turn over to a table with their Chart/Table radios. Only the face showing is visible to keyboards and screen readers.
  • Chips and buttons that held focus are replaced after each reply, so focus moves to the newest chip, or the message box if there isn’t one.
  • Reduced motion removes the typing delay and turns off message, row, drawer and disclosure animations, and chart animation.
